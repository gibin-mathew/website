Finding social handles for 3.4 million companies

Researchers were collecting companies' official social media handles, on Twitter and other networks, by visiting company websites one at a time. Across S&P's company universe that meant millions of visits.

I wrote a Python and Selenium crawler. It ran across 3.4M+ company websites. Two things made it usable rather than just big. Domain resolution made sure it hit the right site for each company. De-duplication left each company with one clean answer.

It replaced about 160 FTE of manual research and fed a data product with $1.1M of potential revenue. It was the largest of my S&P automations, which together came to about 168 FTE of capacity.

A filings crawler across 40+ state portals

Financial filings were spread across U.S. state portals, each with its own search, its own layout and its own PDFs. I built a crawler that covered 40+ of them: it found the filings, downloaded the PDFs, pulled the text out with pypdf and PDFMiner, and translated regional filings. It saved close to 3 FTE.

It's also where I learned the lesson behind my later work. Each portal had its own script, and every redesign on any one of them meant a code fix, a test and a deployment. That's the problem the config-driven RPA engine solves, and I wrote about the shift in why I stopped writing one bot per website.
